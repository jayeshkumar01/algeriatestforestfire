Process

From field reading to FWI score

Temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, rainfall, and fuel-moisture codes (FFMC, DMC, ISI) for a given day and region, are standardized using the same scikit-learn StandardScaler fitted during training, then passed through the Ridge regression model for a continuous Fire Weather Index score.

01

Enter conditions

Temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, rainfall, and fuel-moisture codes (FFMC, DMC, ISI) for a given day and region.

02

Inputs are standardized

Values are scaled using the same scikit-learn StandardScaler fitted during training, so every feature is on comparable ground.

03

Ridge regression scores it

The trained model returns a Fire Weather Index — a continuous measure of fire intensity potential for that day.

Models

Two complementary approaches

The RidgeCV regression predicts a continuous FWI score, while the Decision Tree classifier makes binary predictions (Fire / Not fire) with interpretable depth.

01

FWI Score (Continuous)

RidgeCV regression predicts a Fire Weather Index from 9 features. Output ranges ~0–30+, with higher values indicating greater fire risk.

02

Fire Risk (Binary)

Decision Tree with max_depth=3 predicts fire vs. not-fire from 10 features (includes DC and BUI). Simple, fast, and interpretable.

03

Both regions

Models are trained on Bejaia and Sidi Bel-abbes data, with Region as an input feature.
